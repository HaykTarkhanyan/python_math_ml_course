\begin{frame}{Open the black box --- from any model}
  Deck 1 read the model directly (coefficients, tree splits). But a random forest, a boosting
  model, a neural net --- you \emph{can't} read those.
  \vfill
  \textbf{Model-agnostic} tools work on \emph{any} fitted model and answer two questions:
  \begin{itemize}\small
    \item \textbf{How much} does a feature matter? $\to$ \textbf{Permutation Feature Importance (PFI)}.
    \item \textbf{How} does a feature act on the prediction? $\to$ \textbf{feature effects (PDP / ICE)}.
  \end{itemize}
  \vfill
  \footnotesize Running example continues: a \textbf{bike-sharing} random forest (deck 1's data).
\end{frame}

\section{Permutation feature importance}

\begin{frame}[plain]
  \vfill
  \begin{center}
    {\Large\color{popblue}\textbf{How much does a feature matter?}}\\[0.9em]
    \begin{minipage}{0.82\textwidth}\centering\small
      Make a feature useless and watch the error rise.
    \end{minipage}
  \end{center}
  \vfill
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Permutation feature importance: the idea}
  \textbf{Make feature $X_j$ useless} by \textbf{permuting} its column (shuffle its values across
  rows): same marginal distribution, but its link to the target is broken. Then see how much the
  error rises:
  \[
    \mathrm{PFI}_j \;=\; \underbrace{\mathbb{E}\big[L(\hat f(\text{$X_j$ permuted}), Y)\big]}_{\text{error after}}
    \;-\; \underbrace{\mathbb{E}\big[L(\hat f(X), Y)\big]}_{\text{baseline error}}.
  \]
  \begin{itemize}\small
    \item Big rise $\Rightarrow$ the model leaned on $X_j$. Near zero $\Rightarrow$ it didn't.
    \item \textbf{Permute, don't drop} --- the model is already trained; we can't remove a feature
      without refitting a \emph{different} model.
    \item Repeat the shuffle several times and average (it's random).
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Train or test? They answer different questions}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \column{0.5\textwidth}
    \textbf{Predict-first:} an \texttt{xgboost} model overfits noise. Which features does PFI flag
    --- on train vs.\ on test?
    \vskip 4pt
    \pause
    \begin{itemize}\small
      \item \textbf{Train PFI} highlights whatever the model \emph{overfit} to (spurious links).
      \item \textbf{Test PFI} shows what actually \emph{generalizes} --- here, nothing.
    \end{itemize}
    \vskip 4pt
    \fcolorbox{armred}{armred!8}{\parbox{0.96\linewidth}{\small
      For ``which features help the model generalize?'', compute PFI on \textbf{test} data.}}
    \column{0.5\textwidth}
    \begin{center}
      \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig/pfi_test_vs_train.pdf}\\[-2pt]
      \scriptsize Illustrative example (not the bike model). Source: LMU IML (CC BY 4.0).
    \end{center}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The correlation trap}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \column{0.5\textwidth}
    Permuting a feature that is \textbf{correlated} with others creates \textbf{impossible rows}
    (e.g.\ a hot \texttt{temp} with a freezing \texttt{atemp}).
    \vskip 4pt
    \begin{itemize}\small
      \item The model is then scored on inputs it \emph{never sees} in reality (extrapolation).
      \item $\Rightarrow$ PFI can \textbf{inflate} the importance of correlated features.
    \end{itemize}
    \vskip 4pt
    \footnotesize \textbf{Note the flip:} correlation \emph{deflated} impurity importance (deck 1);
    here it \emph{inflates} PFI. \quad \textbf{Next three slides:} two repairs --- permuting more
    carefully, and not permuting at all --- and what they reveal about \texttt{temp}/\texttt{atemp}.
    \column{0.5\textwidth}
    \begin{center}
      \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig/trap_temp_atemp.pdf}\\[-2pt]
      \scriptsize Bike: \texttt{temp}/\texttt{atemp} corr.\ $0.99$; permuting \texttt{temp} makes impossible rows.
    \end{center}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Repair 1 --- Conditional Feature Importance (CFI)}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \column{0.54\textwidth}
    The trap came from permuting \textbf{freely}. So don't: permute $X_j$ only \textbf{among rows
    that look alike on the other features} --- that is the ``\textbf{conditional}'' in the name.
    \vskip 4pt
    \begin{itemize}\small
      \item Group the rows by $\xv_{-j}$ (e.g.\ the leaves of a small tree predicting $X_j$ from
        the rest), then shuffle $X_j$ \emph{inside each group}.
      \item Warm days get swapped with warm days $\Rightarrow$ no freezing-\texttt{atemp} /
        hot-\texttt{temp} monsters.
    \end{itemize}
    \vskip 4pt
    \fcolorbox{armblue}{armblue!8}{\parbox{0.96\linewidth}{\small
      \textbf{It answers a different question.}
      \textbf{PFI:} ``does the model \emph{use} this feature?''
      \textbf{CFI:} ``does this feature carry information \emph{beyond what the others already
      give me}?''}}
    \column{0.46\textwidth}
    \fcolorbox{armorange}{armorange!12}{\parbox{0.96\linewidth}{\small
      \textbf{Consequence to expect:} for two near-duplicate features, CFI sends \textbf{both}
      toward zero --- each is redundant \emph{given} the other. That is correct for the question
      CFI asks, and badly misleading if you read it as ``temperature doesn't matter.''}}
    \vskip 8pt
    \footnotesize On bike this drops \texttt{temp} from $284$ to $\mathbf{18}$ and \texttt{atemp}
    from $413$ to $\mathbf{39}$ MAE --- while \texttt{yr}, which nothing can stand in for, barely
    moves ($766 \to 671$).
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Repair 2 --- Leave-One-Covariate-Out (LOCO)}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \column{0.54\textwidth}
    Deck's earlier warning was ``we can't drop a feature without refitting a \emph{different}
    model.'' \textbf{Leave-One-Covariate-Out} (\textbf{LOCO}; Lei et al., 2018) says: fine,
    \textbf{refit}.
    \[
      \mathrm{LOCO}_j \;=\; \underbrace{L\big(\hat f_{-j}\big)}_{\text{model retrained without } j}
      \;-\; \underbrace{L\big(\hat f\big)}_{\text{full model}}
    \]
    \begin{itemize}\small
      \item No permuting at all $\Rightarrow$ \textbf{no impossible rows, ever}.
      \item Cost: \textbf{$p$ refits} instead of $p$ shuffles.
      \item Question asked: ``would I lose anything by \textbf{never collecting} this feature?''
    \end{itemize}
    \column{0.46\textwidth}
    \fcolorbox{armred}{armred!8}{\parbox{0.96\linewidth}{\small
      \textbf{The bike headline:} PFI scores \texttt{atemp} at $413$ --- \textbf{second only to
      \texttt{yr}} --- yet $\mathrm{LOCO}(\texttt{atemp}) = \mathbf{-0.7}$ MAE: drop it and the
      model gets \emph{very slightly better}.
      \vskip 3pt
      Nothing is broken --- \texttt{temp} simply carries the same signal. \textbf{``Important''
      and ``needed'' are different words.}}}
    \vskip 6pt
    \footnotesize LOCO can go \textbf{negative}: removing a redundant feature can help by
    shortening the model's search.
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Three questions, three answers}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \column{0.40\textwidth}
    All three are measured in the \textbf{same unit} --- rise in test MAE --- so you can read them
    off one axis:
    \vskip 4pt
    \begin{itemize}\small
      \item \texttt{yr}: all three agree ($766 / 671 / 653$). \textbf{Nothing substitutes for it.}
      \item \texttt{temp}, \texttt{atemp}: huge under PFI, $\approx 0$ under CFI and LOCO.
        \textbf{Each is only redundant because the other exists.}
    \end{itemize}
    \vskip 4pt
    \fcolorbox{paramgreen}{paramgreen!8}{\parbox{0.96\linewidth}{\small
      A feature scoring high on PFI but $\approx 0$ on LOCO is the signature of a
      \textbf{duplicated signal} --- not of an unimportant feature.}}
    \column{0.60\textwidth}
    \begin{center}
      \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig/pfi_cfi_loco_bike.pdf}
    \end{center}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{And one more: SAGE, the fair split of performance}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \column{0.56\textwidth}
    PFI, CFI and LOCO each remove \textbf{one} feature at a time --- which is exactly why
    duplicated features confuse them. \textbf{SAGE} (\emph{Shapley Additive Global importancE};
    Covert et al., 2020) removes features in \textbf{every possible subset} and gives each one its
    \emph{average} contribution to the \textbf{loss}.
    \vskip 4pt
    \begin{itemize}\small
      \item That averaging-over-subsets is the \textbf{Shapley} idea --- the whole of deck 3, but
        applied to the model's \emph{error} instead of a single prediction.
      \item Because it is a fair split, the scores \textbf{add up} to the model's total loss
        reduction: importance as \emph{shares of performance}.
      \item Redundant features \textbf{share} the credit rather than each looking essential
        (PFI) or each looking worthless (CFI/LOCO).
    \end{itemize}
    \column{0.44\textwidth}
    \fcolorbox{armorange}{armorange!12}{\parbox{0.96\linewidth}{\small
      \textbf{The catch:} $2^p$ subsets. SAGE is \textbf{sampled}, not exact, and is by far the
      most expensive method in this deck. Library: \texttt{sage-importance}.}}
    \vskip 8pt
    \fcolorbox{armblue}{armblue!8}{\parbox{0.96\linewidth}{\small
      \textbf{Pick by question, not by fashion:}
      \vskip 3pt
      \begin{tabular}{@{}l@{\ }l@{}}
        \textbf{PFI} & does the model use it? \\
        \textbf{CFI} & does it add new info? \\
        \textbf{LOCO} & do I need to collect it? \\
        \textbf{SAGE} & what is its fair share? \\
      \end{tabular}}}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Important $\neq$ causal}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \column{0.46\textwidth}
    Summer heat drives both \textbf{ice-cream sales} and \textbf{drownings}. A model predicting
    drownings from ice-cream sales finds ice cream \textbf{highly important}.
    \vskip 6pt
    \begin{itemize}\small
      \item PFI is right: the \emph{model} relies on ice cream.
      \item But \textbf{banning ice cream won't stop drownings} --- it isn't the cause.
    \end{itemize}
    \vskip 4pt
    \fcolorbox{armred}{armred!8}{\parbox{0.96\linewidth}{\small
      Importance tells you what the \textbf{model uses}, not what \emph{causes} the outcome.}}
    \vskip 4pt
    {\footnotesize \textbf{In our data:} \texttt{yr} is the \#1 feature, yet the year doesn't
    \emph{cause} rides --- it proxies the service growing. \emph{(Confounding --- a different problem
    from the trap's impossible inputs.)}}
    \column{0.54\textwidth}
    \begin{center}
      \begin{tikzpicture}[font=\small, >=stealth, node distance=1.1cm,
        v/.style={draw, ellipse, inner sep=2pt, minimum width=1.9cm}]

      \scriptsize Model uses the \emph{proxy} (ice cream), not the cause (temperature).
    \end{center}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\section{Feature effects: PDP \& ICE}

\begin{frame}[plain]
  \vfill
  \begin{center}
    {\Large\color{popblue}\textbf{From ``how much'' to ``how''}}\\[0.9em]
    \begin{minipage}{0.82\textwidth}\centering\small
      Importance is one number; effects show the \emph{shape} of a feature's influence.
    \end{minipage}
  \end{center}
  \vfill
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Functional ANOVA: decomposing a prediction}
  Deck 1 introduced \textbf{main effects} and \textbf{interactions} in words. Here is the same
  split, written down: a baseline, each feature acting \emph{alone}, then features acting
  \emph{together}. It is the \textbf{functional ANOVA} (\textbf{fANOVA}) decomposition
  (Hooker, 2004):
  \[
    \hat f(\xv) \;=\; \underbrace{g_\emptyset}_{\text{baseline}}
      \;+\; \underbrace{\textstyle\sum_j g_j(x_j)}_{\text{main effects}}
      \;+\; \underbrace{\textstyle\sum_{j<k} g_{jk}(x_j,x_k)}_{\text{2-way interactions}}
      \;+\;\cdots
  \]
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \column{0.5\textwidth}
    \fcolorbox{armblue}{armblue!8}{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\small
      \textbf{Main effect} $g_j$: how the prediction moves as $x_j$ varies \emph{alone}
      (averaging the rest).}}
    \column{0.5\textwidth}
    \fcolorbox{armorange}{armorange!12}{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\small
      \textbf{Interaction} $g_{jk}$: the leftover when $x_j$'s effect \emph{depends on} $x_k$.
      Zero for an \textbf{additive} model (linear with no product terms, deck 1).}}
  \end{columns}
  \vskip 6pt
  \fcolorbox{paramgreen}{paramgreen!8}{\parbox{0.97\linewidth}{\small
    \textbf{This one equation organises the whole deck:} the effect plots estimate the
    $g_j$, and the interaction measures put a number on the $g_{jk}$.}}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Computing the components --- and where the PDP fits}
  Work \textbf{order by order}: average out everything not in $S$, then subtract what the
  lower orders already explained.
  \[
    g_S(\xv_S) \;=\; \underbrace{\hat f_{S,\mathrm{PD}}(\xv_S)}_{\text{average out all features not in } S}
    \;-\; \sum_{V \subsetneq S} g_V(\xv_V)
  \]
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \column{0.52\textwidth}
    {\small Unrolling the recursion:}
    \begin{align*}
      g_\emptyset &= \mathbb{E}[\hat f(X)] \\
      g_j(x_j) &= \hat f_{j,\mathrm{PD}}(x_j) - g_\emptyset \\
      g_{jk}(x_j,x_k) &= \hat f_{jk,\mathrm{PD}} - g_j - g_k - g_\emptyset
    \end{align*}
    \column{0.48\textwidth}
    \fcolorbox{armblue}{armblue!8}{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\small
      Read the second line again: the \textbf{centred PDP \emph{is}} the fANOVA main effect $g_j$
      --- not an approximation of it. That is why the PDP is \emph{the} main-effect plot.}}
  \end{columns}
  \vskip 6pt
  \fcolorbox{armorange}{armorange!12}{\parbox{0.97\linewidth}{\small
    \textbf{Two catches:} the full decomposition needs $2^p$ PD-functions (hopeless past a
    handful of features), and the clean split \textbf{assumes independent features} --- our
    \texttt{temp}/\texttt{atemp} problem again.}}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Why ``ANOVA''? Because it splits the variance}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \column{0.54\textwidth}
    Classical \textbf{ANOVA} splits the variance of an \emph{outcome} into main effects plus
    interactions. \textbf{fANOVA} does the same for the variance of a fitted \emph{function}'s
    predictions.
    \vskip 4pt
    If the features are \textbf{independent}, the components are uncorrelated, so the variance
    splits with \textbf{no covariance terms}:
    \[ \var[\hat f(X)] = \sum_{S} \var[g_S(X_S)] \]
    Divide through by $\var[\hat f(X)]$ and the pieces are \textbf{fractions that sum to 1}.
    \column{0.46\textwidth}
    \fcolorbox{armblue}{armblue!8}{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\small
      \textbf{Sobol index} --- the share of prediction variance explained by one component:
      \[ S_V = \frac{\var[g_V(X_V)]}{\var[\hat f(X)]} \]
      A main-effect $S_j$ answers ``how much of the model's variability is feature $j$ alone?'';
      the higher-order ones are what is left for pairs, triples, \dots}}
  \end{columns}
  \vskip 4pt
  \fcolorbox{paramgreen}{paramgreen!8}{\parbox{0.97\linewidth}{\small
    So ``importance'' can also mean \textbf{share of variance} --- yet another definition, on top
    of deck 1's coefficients/impurity and this deck's PFI. \textbf{Always say which one you mean.}}}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Start local: one curve per observation (ICE)}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \column{0.42\textwidth}
    Take \textbf{one day}. Hold everything about it fixed, sweep \texttt{temp} across its range,
    and record what the model predicts. That is one \textbf{individual conditional expectation}
    (\textbf{ICE}) curve --- a what-if for that single day (Goldstein et al., 2015).
    \vskip 4pt
    Do it for every day $\Rightarrow$ one curve each.
    \vskip 4pt
    \begin{itemize}\small
      \item The \textbf{level} differs: some days are busy, some quiet.
      \item The \textbf{shape} is what we care about --- here almost every curve steps up around
        \texttt{temp}$\approx 0.45$.
    \end{itemize}
    \column{0.58\textwidth}
    \begin{center}
      \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig/ice_bike_temp.pdf}
    \end{center}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Average the ICE curves --- and that \emph{is} the PDP}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \column{0.42\textwidth}
    At each value of \texttt{temp}, take the \textbf{mean over all the curves}. The result is the
    \textbf{partial dependence plot}.
    \[ \hat f_{j,\mathrm{PD}}(x_j) = \frac1n \sum_{i=1}^{n} \hat f\big(x_j,\ \xv^{(i)}_{-j}\big) \]
    \begin{itemize}\small
      \item Centre it and you have $g_j$ --- the \textbf{fANOVA main effect} from two slides ago.
      \item One curve replaces $731$: readable, but everything the individual curves disagreed
        about is now \textbf{gone}.
    \end{itemize}
    \column{0.58\textwidth}
    \begin{center}
      \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig/ice_pdp_bike_temp.pdf}
    \end{center}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{What the average can hide}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \column{0.46\textwidth}
    \textbf{Predict-first:} this PDP (orange) is \textbf{flat}. Does the feature not matter?
    \vskip 4pt
    \pause
    \textbf{No.} The ICE curves \textbf{fan out}: $x$'s effect \textbf{flips sign depending on
    another feature} --- rising for half the rows, falling for the other half, so the opposing
    effects \textbf{cancel in the average}. The PDP hid that interaction; ICE exposes it --- exactly
    the kind of interaction a \emph{linear} model couldn't represent (deck 1).
    \column{0.54\textwidth}
    \begin{center}
      \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig/ice_interaction_toy.pdf}
    \end{center}
  \end{columns}
  \vskip 2pt
  \footnotesize \textbf{The visual test:} \emph{parallel} ICE curves $=$ no interaction; curves that
  \textbf{fan out} $=$ interaction. \textbf{Always look at the ICE before trusting a PDP.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{PDP shares PFI's correlation trap --- and ALE fixes it}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \column{0.5\textwidth}
    Look again at the averaging formula: it pairs \emph{every} value of \texttt{temp} with
    \emph{every} other row's features. With correlated features that builds the same
    \textbf{impossible rows} as the PFI trap --- a hot \texttt{temp} on a freezing-\texttt{atemp} day.
    \vskip 6pt
    \textbf{ALE} (Accumulated Local Effects; Apley \& Zhu, 2020) fixes it: split the feature into
    small windows, and inside each window ask only how the prediction changes for the rows
    \emph{that are actually there}. Accumulate those local changes $\Rightarrow$ a
    correlation-robust effect curve that never leaves the data.
    \column{0.5\textwidth}
    \begin{center}
      \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig/ale_vs_pdp_bike.pdf}\\[-2pt]
      \scriptsize Bike \texttt{temp}: PDP overshoots at the extremes; ALE stays realistic.
    \end{center}
  \end{columns}
  \vskip 2pt
  \footnotesize But there is an obvious middle option we skipped --- and it is a trap. Next slide.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Three ways to hold the other features ``fixed''}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \column{0.42\textwidth}
    {\small
    \textbf{PDP} --- average over \emph{all} rows:
    \[ \mathbb{E}_{X_{-j}}\big[\hat f(x_j, X_{-j})\big] \]
    Builds impossible rows. \textcolor{armred}{\textbf{Extrapolates.}}
    \vskip 4pt
    \textbf{M-plot} --- the obvious fix: average only over rows that \emph{really have} that $x_j$:
    \[ \mathbb{E}\big[\hat f(X) \mid X_j = x_j\big] \]
    Never extrapolates --- but warm days also have high \texttt{atemp}, \texttt{season},
    \texttt{mnth}, so their effects get charged to \texttt{temp}.
    \textcolor{armred}{\textbf{Confounded.}}
    \vskip 4pt
    \textbf{ALE} --- inside each window take the \emph{difference} in prediction; differencing
    cancels the neighbours' contribution. \textcolor{paramgreen}{\textbf{Neither problem.}}}
    \column{0.58\textwidth}
    \begin{center}
      \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig/pdp_mplot_ale_bike.pdf}
    \end{center}
    \vskip -1pt
    {\scriptsize The M-plot spans $\approx 4200$ rentals against ALE's $\approx 1600$: most of that
    steepness belongs to the \emph{other} features, not to \texttt{temp}.}
    \vskip 4pt
    \fcolorbox{paramgreen}{paramgreen!8}{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\footnotesize
      \textbf{Correlated features $\Rightarrow$ use ALE.}}}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\section{Feature interactions}

\begin{frame}[plain]
  \vfill
  \begin{center}
    {\Large\color{popblue}\textbf{When one feature's effect depends on another}}\\[0.9em]
    \begin{minipage}{0.82\textwidth}\centering\small
      \emph{See} it (the 2-D PDP), then put a \emph{number} on it (the H-statistic).
    \end{minipage}
  \end{center}
  \vfill
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Seeing an interaction: the 2-D PDP}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \column{0.46\textwidth}
    Vary \textbf{two} features together and average over the rest --- a partial-dependence
    \textbf{surface}.
    \vskip 4pt
    \begin{itemize}\small
      \item \textbf{No interaction} $\Rightarrow$ the bands are \textbf{parallel}: one feature's
        effect is the same at every value of the other.
      \item \textbf{Interaction} $\Rightarrow$ the bands \textbf{fan / bend}: here $x_1$'s effect
        \textbf{reverses} with the sign of $x_2$ (a clean $y=x_1x_2$ toy).
    \end{itemize}
    \vskip 4pt
    \fcolorbox{armorange}{armorange!12}{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\small
      Illustrative on purpose --- the bike model's \emph{real} interactions are mild (we measure them
      next).}}
    \column{0.54\textwidth}
    \begin{center}
      \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig/pdp_2d_interaction.pdf}
    \end{center}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{First: what exactly counts as ``no interaction''?}
  To compare effects we first put them on the same footing --- \textbf{centre} every PD-function by
  subtracting the baseline: $\;\hat f^{\,c}_{S,\mathrm{PD}} = \hat f_{S,\mathrm{PD}} - g_\emptyset$.
  \vskip 4pt
  \fcolorbox{armblue}{armblue!8}{\parbox{0.97\linewidth}{\small
    \textbf{Definition.} $x_j$ and $x_k$ \textbf{do not interact} if their joint effect is just the
    two separate effects added up:
    \[ \hat f^{\,c}_{jk,\mathrm{PD}}(x_j,x_k) \;=\; \hat f^{\,c}_{j,\mathrm{PD}}(x_j)
       \;+\; \hat f^{\,c}_{k,\mathrm{PD}}(x_k) \]}}
  \vskip 6pt
  So the \textbf{interaction is whatever is left over} when you subtract the two main effects from
  the joint one:
  \[
    \underbrace{\hat f^{\,c}_{jk,\mathrm{PD}}(x_j,x_k)
      - \hat f^{\,c}_{j,\mathrm{PD}}(x_j) - \hat f^{\,c}_{k,\mathrm{PD}}(x_k)}_{
      \textstyle =\; g_{jk}(x_j,x_k)\ \text{ --- the fANOVA interaction component}}
  \]
  \vskip 2pt
  \footnotesize For an \textbf{additive} model this leftover is $0$ at every point. All that remains
  is to turn ``how big is the leftover?'' into one number.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Friedman's H: the leftover as a fraction}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \column{0.52\textwidth}
    Take the \textbf{variance of the leftover}, relative to the variance of the joint effect
    (Friedman \& Popescu, 2008 --- \emph{the same paper as RuleFit}, deck 1):
    \[
      H^2_{jk} = \frac{\var\big[\hat f^{\,c}_{jk} - \hat f^{\,c}_{j} - \hat f^{\,c}_{k}\big]}
                      {\var\big[\hat f^{\,c}_{jk}\big]}
    \]
    In practice, the sum over the rows:
    \[
      H^2_{jk} = \frac{\sum_i \big(\hat f^{\,c}_{jk} - \hat f^{\,c}_{j} - \hat f^{\,c}_{k}\big)^2}
      {\sum_i \big(\hat f^{\,c}_{jk}\big)^2}
      \quad \text{\footnotesize (at each row $i$)}
    \]
    \vskip 2pt
    {\small Dividing by the joint effect makes it a \textbf{share}, so $H \in [0,1]$ and pairs are
    comparable: $0 =$ perfectly additive, $\to 1 =$ almost all interaction.}
    \column{0.48\textwidth}
    \begin{center}
      \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig/h_statistic_bar.pdf}
    \end{center}
    \vskip -1pt
    {\scriptsize All $H < 0.2$: the bike model is \textbf{mostly main effects};
    \texttt{temp}$\times$\texttt{season} ($H{=}0.19$) leads, but modestly.}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Does a feature interact with \emph{anything}? ($H_j$) --- and the caveats}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \column{0.5\textwidth}
    Same construction, but split the world into \textbf{feature $j$} and \textbf{everything else}
    $(-j)$ instead of two single features:
    \[
      H^2_{j} = \frac{\sum_i \big(\hat f^{\,c}(\xv^{(i)})
        - \hat f^{\,c}_{j}(x^{(i)}_j) - \hat f^{\,c}_{-j}(\xv^{(i)}_{-j})\big)^2}
      {\sum_i \big(\hat f^{\,c}(\xv^{(i)})\big)^2}
    \]
    One number per feature: \emph{``is this feature additive, or does it depend on the rest of the
    row?''} On bike, \texttt{temp} leads at $0.21$ --- the same feature whose curve bends (deck 1).
    \vskip 4pt
    \fcolorbox{armorange}{armorange!12}{\parbox{0.96\linewidth}{\scriptsize
      \textbf{Caveats.} \textbf{(1)} Costly: a $k$-way $H$ needs $\approx 2^k$ PD-functions.
      \textbf{(2)} \textbf{Correlation inflates it} --- we dropped \texttt{atemp} for that reason.
      \textbf{(3)} \textbf{No natural threshold} --- read $H$ as a \emph{ranking}.
      \textbf{(4)} Not in \texttt{sklearn}: use \texttt{pyartemis}.}}
    \column{0.5\textwidth}
    \begin{center}
      \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig/h_overall_bar.pdf}
    \end{center}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\end{frame}

\section*{Wrap-up}

\begin{frame}{Recap}
  \begin{itemize}\small
    \item \textbf{Model-agnostic} tools open any black box.
    \item \textbf{PFI} (\emph{how much}): permute a feature, measure the error rise, on \textbf{test}
      data. Watch the \textbf{correlation trap}, and remember \textbf{important $\neq$ causal}.
    \item Four methods, \textbf{four different questions}: \textbf{PFI} (does the model use it?),
      \textbf{CFI} (new info beyond the others?), \textbf{LOCO} (must I collect it?), \textbf{SAGE}
      (fair share). Bike: \texttt{atemp} $= 413$ on PFI, $\mathbf{-0.7}$ on LOCO.
    \item \textbf{fANOVA} (Hooker, 2004) organises it all: prediction $=$ baseline $+$ main effects
      $+$ interactions, with $g_j = $ the \textbf{centred PDP}; under independence it splits the
      \textbf{prediction variance} (the \textbf{Sobol index}).
    \item \textbf{ICE} (\emph{how}): one what-if curve per row. \textbf{Average them and that
      \emph{is} the PDP} --- readable, but it can hide a sign-flipping interaction. Of the three
      ways to average, \textbf{PDP} extrapolates and \textbf{M-plots} confound, so with correlated
      features use \textbf{ALE} (Apley \& Zhu, 2020).
    \item The \textbf{2-D PDP} \emph{shows} an interaction; \textbf{Friedman's H} \emph{measures} it
      as the \textbf{leftover after removing the main effects}: pairwise $H_{jk}$, or $H_j$ for
      ``does $j$ interact with anything?'' (bike: mostly main effects).
  \end{itemize}
  \vfill
  \fcolorbox{paramgreen}{paramgreen!8}{\parbox{0.97\linewidth}{\small
    \textbf{Next:} \textbf{SHAP} --- one method for a \emph{single} prediction that also rolls up
    to global importance and effects.}}
\end{frame}
