\section{One space for words and pictures}

\begin{frame}[plain]
  \vfill
  \begin{center}
    {\Large\color{popblue}\textbf{One space for words and pictures}}\\[0.9em]
    \begin{minipage}{0.82\textwidth}\centering\small
      Before we can steer with text, text and images must live somewhere in common.
    \end{minipage}
  \end{center}
  \vfill
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{CLIP: two encoders, one destination}
  \small
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \column{0.5\textwidth}
    \begin{center}
      \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{wl_clip_encoders.jpg}\\[2pt]
      {\scriptsize\color{gray} Welch Labs / 3Blue1Brown (2025)}
    \end{center}
    \column{0.5\textwidth}
    \vskip 4pt
    \textbf{CLIP} - \textbf{Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training} (Radford et al., 2021)
    - is two separate networks:
    \begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{2pt}
      \item a \textbf{text} encoder: caption $\to$ vector
      \item an \textbf{image} encoder: picture $\to$ vector
    \end{itemize}
    \vskip 4pt
    Both output vectors of the same length (512 in the original), into the same space.
    \vskip 5pt
    \fcolorbox{armblue}{armblue!8}{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\footnotesize
      The goal: an image and \emph{its own caption} should land in nearly the same place.
      Trained on 400 million image-caption pairs scraped from the web.}}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Contrastive learning, in one idea}
  \small
  We have no labels saying "this vector should be at coordinates $(0.3, -1.1, \dots)$".
  All we have is which caption belongs to which image. That is enough.
  \vskip 6pt
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \column{0.5\textwidth}
    \fcolorbox{armblue}{armblue!8}{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\small
      \textbf{Pull together} what belongs together. \textbf{Push apart} everything else.}}
    \vskip 6pt
    \footnotesize
    Nobody says where anything goes. The geometry is a \emph{consequence} of thousands of
    these pulls and pushes settling against each other.
    \column{0.5\textwidth}
    \fcolorbox{paramgreen}{paramgreen!8}{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\footnotesize
      \textbf{Where the negatives come from is the clever part.} Take a batch of $N$ pairs.
      Each image has one correct caption and $N-1$ wrong ones - already in the batch, free.
      \vskip 3pt
      Bigger batches mean harder negatives, which is one reason CLIP needed to be trained
      at scale.}}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The training objective is a matrix}
  \small
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \column{0.46\textwidth}
    \begin{center}
      \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{wl_clip_matrix.jpg}\\[2pt]
      {\scriptsize\color{gray} Welch Labs / 3Blue1Brown (2025)}
    \end{center}
    \column{0.54\textwidth}
    \footnotesize
    Images as columns, captions as rows. Every cell is the similarity of one image with one
    caption.
    \vskip 5pt
    \begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{2pt}
      \item the \textbf{diagonal} holds the true pairs $\to$ maximize
      \item everything \textbf{off-diagonal} is a mismatch $\to$ minimize
    \end{itemize}
    \vskip 5pt
    \fcolorbox{armblue}{armblue!8}{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\footnotesize
      That is the whole loss: a classification problem where the "classes" are "which of
      these $N$ captions is mine?", run in both directions at once.
      \vskip 3pt
      The \textbf{C} in CLIP is this contrast.}}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The similarity is one you already know}
  \small
  CLIP scores a pair with \textbf{cosine similarity} - the cosine of the angle between the
  two vectors:
  \[
    \mathrm{sim}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})
      = \frac{\mathbf{u}^\top \mathbf{v}}{\|\mathbf{u}\|\,\|\mathbf{v}\|}
      \;=\; \cos\vartheta
  \]
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \column{0.52\textwidth}
    \fcolorbox{armblue}{armblue!8}{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\footnotesize
      \textbf{This is the dot product from L24}, normalized. There we used it to ask how
      much one token should attend to another; here we ask how much a caption matches a
      picture. Same operation, same meaning: \emph{alignment}.}}
    \column{0.48\textwidth}
    \fcolorbox{armorange}{armorange!12}{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\footnotesize
      Dividing out the magnitudes means only \textbf{direction} carries meaning. Two
      vectors pointing the same way are "the same idea", regardless of length.}}
  \end{columns}
  \vskip 5pt
  \footnotesize
  \textcolor{gray}{You met cosine once before as a distance option in clustering (L13).
  This is where it starts earning its keep.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The space learned arithmetic nobody asked for}
  \small
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \column{0.5\textwidth}
    \begin{center}
      \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{wl_clip_hat.jpg}\\[2pt]
      {\scriptsize\color{gray} Welch Labs / 3Blue1Brown (2025)}
    \end{center}
    \column{0.5\textwidth}
    \footnotesize
    Encode a photo of a person \textbf{wearing a hat}. Encode the same person
    \textbf{without}. Subtract the two image vectors.
    \vskip 4pt
    Now search the \emph{text} side for the caption closest to that difference.
    \vskip 5pt
    \fcolorbox{paramgreen}{paramgreen!8}{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\footnotesize
      The top match is "\textbf{hat}" ($0.165$), then "cap", then "helmet".
      \vskip 3pt
      Nothing in the training objective asked for this. Pull matches together, push
      mismatches apart, and a space falls out where \textbf{directions correspond to
      concepts} - and where you can cross from images to words and back.}}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Two consequences}
  \small
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \column{0.5\textwidth}
    \fcolorbox{armblue}{armblue!8}{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\small
      \textbf{Classification for free.} Encode an image. Encode "a photo of a \{cat, dog,
      truck, \dots\}". Pick the caption with the highest similarity.
      \vskip 3pt
      No training on those classes - \textbf{zero-shot}. CLIP was competitive with
      supervised models on datasets it had never been fitted to.}}
    \column{0.5\textwidth}
    \fcolorbox{armred}{armred!8}{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\small
      \textbf{But it only goes one way.} Both encoders map \emph{into} the space. Neither
      comes back out.
      \vskip 3pt
      CLIP can tell you an image matches "a lone tree in the desert". It cannot draw one.}}
  \end{columns}
  \vskip 8pt
  \begin{center}\small
    We have a model that turns pictures into meaning, and a model that turns noise into
    pictures. \textcolor{paramgreen}{\textbf{Bolt them together.}}
  \end{center}
\end{frame}

\section{Conditioning}

\begin{frame}[plain]
  \vfill
  \begin{center}
    {\Large\color{popblue}\textbf{Conditioning}}\\[0.9em]
    \begin{minipage}{0.82\textwidth}\centering\small
      Getting the prompt inside the network. The easy half.
    \end{minipage}
  \end{center}
  \vfill
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Give the denoiser one more input}
  \small
  The network so far is $\eps_{\thetav}(\xv_t, t)$. Add the prompt as a third argument:
  \[
    \eps_{\thetav}(\xv_t,\ t,\ \mathbf{c})
  \]
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \column{0.52\textwidth}
    Train exactly as before, on image-caption pairs, with the same
    $\mathcal{L}_{\text{simple}}$ from L28.
    \vskip 5pt
    \fcolorbox{armblue}{armblue!8}{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\footnotesize
      The bet: knowing the image is \emph{of a tree} should help the model predict which
      noise was added to it. Better denoising, and a model that has learned to use text.}}
    \column{0.48\textwidth}
    \fcolorbox{paramgreen}{paramgreen!8}{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\footnotesize
      \textbf{We have done this before.} Conditioning on $t$ in L27 was the same move: an
      extra input that tells the network which situation it is in. Text is just a much
      richer $t$.}}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{What exactly goes in?}
  \small
  The obvious answer - "the CLIP text vector" - is \textbf{not} what production models do.
  \vskip 5pt
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \column{0.5\textwidth}
    \fcolorbox{armred}{armred!8}{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\footnotesize
      A single pooled 512-d vector squeezes the whole sentence into one point. "A dog
      chasing a cat" and "a cat chasing a dog" become nearly identical.}}
    \column{0.5\textwidth}
    \fcolorbox{armblue}{armblue!8}{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\footnotesize
      Stable Diffusion takes the layer \emph{before} the pooling: a \textbf{sequence} of
      per-token vectors, $\mathbf{77\times768}$. One vector per token, not one per
      sentence.}}
  \end{columns}
  \vskip 4pt
  \footnotesize
  \textcolor{gray}{Why 768 and not the 512 from two slides ago: Stable Diffusion uses a
  larger CLIP variant (ViT-L/14, width 768) than the one in the original zero-shot demos
  (ViT-B/32, width 512). The $77$ is CLIP's fixed maximum token count - prompts are padded
  or truncated to it.}
  \vskip 6pt
  \fcolorbox{armorange}{armorange!12}{\parbox{0.985\linewidth}{\footnotesize
    This is not a detail. The mechanism on the next slide \textbf{needs} a sequence to work
    at all - there is nothing to look across if the prompt is a single point.}}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Cross-attention: how the pixels read the prompt}
  \small
  In L24 attention let each token look at every other token, by matching a \textbf{query}
  against \textbf{keys} and taking a weighted sum of \textbf{values}. Same machinery here -
  but the queries and the keys come from different places.
  \vskip 4pt
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \column{0.55\textwidth}
    \begin{center}
      \begin{tikzpicture}[every node/.style={font=\scriptsize}, node distance=0.7cm]
        \node[draw, rounded corners, fill=armblue!12, minimum width=2.3cm, minimum height=0.6cm] (img) {image patch $\to$ \textbf{query}};
        \node[draw, rounded corners, fill=armorange!16, below=1.1cm of img, minimum width=2.3cm, minimum height=0.6cm] (txt) {77 text tokens $\to$ \textbf{keys}, \textbf{values}};
        \node[draw, rounded corners, fill=paramgreen!14, right=2.0cm of img, minimum width=2.1cm, minimum height=0.6cm] (out) {updated patch};
        \draw[-{Latex}, thick] (img) -- (out);
        \draw[-{Latex}, thick, armorange] (txt) -| node[pos=0.25, below, font=\tiny]{which words matter here?} ($(out.south)+(0,-0.0)$);
      \end{tikzpicture}
    \end{center}
    \vskip 4pt
    \footnotesize
    Every patch of the image asks: \emph{which words in this prompt are about me?} A patch
    that is becoming sky attends to "sky"; a patch becoming bark attends to "tree".
    \column{0.45\textwidth}
    \fcolorbox{armblue}{armblue!8}{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\footnotesize
      \textbf{Self}-attention (L24): queries, keys and values all from one sequence.
      \vskip 3pt
      \textbf{Cross}-attention: queries from the image, keys and values from the text.
      That is the only difference.}}
    \vskip 4pt
    \footnotesize
    These layers sit inside the UNet blocks from L28. Simpler alternatives exist -
    concatenating the text vector, or adding it like the time embedding - and many models
    use several routes at once.
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Doing it properly: DALL$\cdot$E 2}
  \small
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \column{0.55\textwidth}
    OpenAI trained a diffusion model to \textbf{invert the CLIP image encoder}: given a CLIP
    embedding, produce an image that would encode to it.
    \vskip 5pt
    CLIP maps pictures to meaning; the diffusion model maps meaning back to pictures. They
    called it \textbf{unCLIP} (Ramesh et al., 2022); the product was \textbf{DALL$\cdot$E 2}.
    \vskip 5pt
    \fcolorbox{paramgreen}{paramgreen!8}{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\footnotesize
      Prompt adherence jumped far beyond anything before it. This is the moment
      text-to-image stopped being a demo.}}
    \column{0.45\textwidth}
    \fcolorbox{armorange}{armorange!12}{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\footnotesize
      \textbf{Stable Diffusion} (Rombach et al., 2022) arrived around the same time from
      Heidelberg, worked differently in an important way - the subject of L31 - and was
      \textbf{open source}, which is why it is the model everyone experiments with.}}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Is that enough?}
  \small
  \textbf{Predict first:} we have a well-trained conditional model. Prompt: \emph{"a lone
  tree standing in the middle of a desert, under the harsh light of midday, casting a long
  shadow on the sand."} What comes out?
  \vskip 4pt
  \pause
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \column{0.44\textwidth}
    \begin{center}
      \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{wl_no_tree.jpg}\\[2pt]
      {\scriptsize\color{gray} Welch Labs / 3Blue1Brown (2025)}
    \end{center}
    \column{0.56\textwidth}
    \vskip 4pt
    \fcolorbox{armred}{armred!8}{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\small
      A desert. Harsh midday light. A long shadow on the sand.
      \vskip 4pt
      \textbf{No tree.}}}
    \vskip 6pt
    \footnotesize
    The model used the prompt - the scene is right. But it is behaving like a model that
    was asked for a \emph{plausible image, given} the text, and plausible desert
    photographs often have no tree in them.
    \vskip 4pt
    Conditioning tells the model what the prompt \emph{is}. It does not make the model
    \textbf{care}.
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\section{Classifier-free guidance}

\begin{frame}[plain]
  \vfill
  \begin{center}
    {\Large\color{popblue}\textbf{Classifier-free guidance}}\\[0.9em]
    \begin{minipage}{0.82\textwidth}\centering\small
      One idea, three lines of code, and the reason your prompts work.
    \end{minipage}
  \end{center}
  \vfill
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Back to the spiral, with labels}
  \small
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \column{0.42\textwidth}
    \begin{center}
      \includegraphics[width=0.86\linewidth]{spiral_classes.pdf}
    \end{center}
    \column{0.58\textwidth}
    \vskip 6pt
    Split the spiral into three regions and call them classes - our stand-in for a prompt.
    Train exactly as in L28, with the class as an extra input.
    \vskip 6pt
    \fcolorbox{armblue}{armblue!8}{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\footnotesize
      The model now has two jobs at once: \textbf{land on the spiral} (all classes) and
      \textbf{land on the right part of it} (this class).}}
    \vskip 6pt
    \footnotesize
    The first job is easy and dominates the loss - most of the error is in getting near the
    data at all. The second is a small correction on top, and it gets drowned out. That is
    the missing tree, in miniature.
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The trick: train one model to be two models}
  \small
  During training, \textbf{throw the label away} on a fraction of examples (around 10-20\%)
  and replace it with a "no class" token.
  \vskip 5pt
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \column{0.5\textwidth}
    \fcolorbox{paramgreen}{paramgreen!8}{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\footnotesize
      One set of weights, two behaviours:
      \begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
        \item $\eps_{\thetav}(\xv_t,t,\mathbf{c})$ - "draw a cat"
        \item $\eps_{\thetav}(\xv_t,t)$ - "draw anything"
      \end{itemize}}}
    \column{0.5\textwidth}
    \footnotesize
    You could train two separate networks. Dropping the label is free and gives the same
    thing, which is why it is universal.
    \vskip 4pt
    \textbf{Ho \& Salimans (2022)} - "classifier-free" because earlier work steered
    diffusion with a separately trained classifier, and this needs none.
  \end{columns}
  \vskip 6pt
  \fcolorbox{armblue}{armblue!8}{\parbox{0.985\linewidth}{\footnotesize
    Now we have both a general direction and a class-specific one, and we can compare them.
    \textbf{The difference between them is the part of the prediction that is about the
    prompt.}}}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Isolate the prompt's contribution, then exaggerate it}
  \small
  \begin{center}
    \includegraphics[width=0.94\linewidth]{spiral_cfg_fields.pdf}
  \end{center}
  \vskip -3pt
  \footnotesize
  Left: without a class, the field points at the spiral in general. Middle: told "cat", it
  leans outward. Right: subtract them and what remains is \textbf{only} the cat-ness -
  the generic "get to the data" part has cancelled out.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The guidance formula}
  \small
  Scale that difference by $\alpha$ and add it back:
  \[
    \boxed{\ \tilde{\eps} \;=\; \eps_{\thetav}(\xv_t,t)
      \;+\; \alpha\,\Big(\eps_{\thetav}(\xv_t,t,\mathbf{c}) - \eps_{\thetav}(\xv_t,t)\Big)\ }
  \]
  Use $\tilde\eps$ in the sampler in place of the model's raw output.
  \vskip 4pt
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \column{0.5\textwidth}
    \fcolorbox{armblue}{armblue!8}{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\footnotesize
      \textbf{Read off the special cases:}
      \begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
        \item $\alpha = 0$: ignore the prompt entirely
        \item $\alpha = \mathbf{1}$: exactly the plain conditional model - \textbf{no
          guidance}
        \item $\alpha > 1$: push further in the prompt's direction than the model itself
          would
      \end{itemize}}}
    \column{0.5\textwidth}
    \fcolorbox{armorange}{armorange!12}{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\footnotesize
      \textbf{Watch the convention.} Some papers put the \emph{conditional} prediction as
      the base term, which shifts what "no guidance" means. In the form above - the one
      \texttt{diffusers} uses - $\alpha=1$ is neutral, and its default
      \texttt{guidance\_scale} is $7.5$.
      \vskip 3pt
      Always check which convention a codebase means before comparing numbers.}}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{By hand: one guidance step}
  \small
  One pixel. The unconditional model predicts $\eps_{\thetav}(\xv_t,t) = 0.90$; told the
  prompt, it predicts $\eps_{\thetav}(\xv_t,t,\mathbf{c}) = 1.15$.
  \vskip 4pt
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \column{0.5\textwidth}
    {\footnotesize
    The prompt's own contribution is the difference:
    \[ 1.15 - 0.90 = 0.25 \]
    Guidance scales \emph{that}, and adds it back:
    \vskip 3pt
    \begin{tabular}{@{}l@{\hskip 8pt}l@{\hskip 6pt}l@{}}
      $\alpha$ & $\tilde\eps = 0.90 + \alpha(0.25)$ & \\\hline
      $0$   & $0.900$ & prompt ignored \\
      $1$   & $1.150$ & \textbf{= the conditional model} \\
      $3$   & $1.650$ & \\
      $7.5$ & $2.775$ & the usual working range \\
    \end{tabular}}
    \column{0.5\textwidth}
    \fcolorbox{armblue}{armblue!8}{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\footnotesize
      Check the $\alpha=1$ row against the formula on the last slide: the base term and
      the subtracted term cancel, leaving exactly
      $\eps_{\thetav}(\xv_t,t,\mathbf{c})$. \textbf{That is why $\alpha=1$ means no
      guidance}, and not $\alpha=0$.}}
    \vskip 5pt
    \fcolorbox{armorange}{armorange!12}{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\footnotesize
      Note how far $\alpha=7.5$ pushes: $2.775$ against a raw prediction of $1.15$. We are
      asking the sampler to remove more than twice the noise the model actually thinks is
      there. It works - but you can see why it eventually stops working.}}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{On the spiral: the cost curve}
  \small
  Sampling the outer class only, at three guidance strengths:
  \begin{center}
    \includegraphics[width=0.86\linewidth]{spiral_cfg_sweep.pdf}
  \end{center}
  \vskip -4pt
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \column{0.5\textwidth}
    \fcolorbox{armblue}{armblue!8}{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\scriptsize
      Three classes split by radius is an \textbf{easy} problem, so conditioning alone
      already lands on the right arm at $\alpha=1$. The toy does not reproduce the missing
      tree - the Stable Diffusion image does that.}}
    \column{0.5\textwidth}
    \fcolorbox{armred}{armred!8}{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\scriptsize
      What it \emph{does} show is overdoing it. At $\alpha=7.5$ the samples \textbf{leave
      the spiral altogether} - mean radius $0.91 \to 1.10$, past the edge of the data.}}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{And on a real model}
  \small
  Same prompt, same weights, same sampler. \textbf{Only $\alpha$ changes.}
  \vskip 3pt
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \column{0.37\textwidth}
    \begin{center}
      \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{wl_alpha_1.jpg}\\[1pt]
      {\scriptsize $\alpha = 1$ - no guidance. Desert, shadow, \textbf{no tree}.}
    \end{center}
    \column{0.37\textwidth}
    \begin{center}
      \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{wl_alpha_8.jpg}\\[1pt]
      {\scriptsize $\alpha = 8$ - the tree is there.}
    \end{center}
    \column{0.26\textwidth}
    \vskip 2pt
    \fcolorbox{paramgreen}{paramgreen!8}{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\footnotesize
      \textbf{We changed one number.} The prompt already pointed at a tree; guidance just
      amplified that direction.}}
    \vskip 4pt
    \footnotesize
    Practically every image model you have used runs with guidance on. It is the
    difference between "vaguely related to your prompt" and "your prompt".
  \end{columns}
  \vskip 2pt
  \begin{center}{\scriptsize\color{gray} Welch Labs / 3Blue1Brown (2025)}\end{center}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Negative prompts: subtract what you do not want}
  \small
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \column{0.5\textwidth}
    Nothing forces the base term to be the \emph{unconditional} prediction. Replace it with
    the prediction for a prompt describing what you want to \textbf{avoid}:
    \[
      \tilde\eps = \eps_{\thetav}(\xv_t,t,\mathbf{c}_{\text{neg}})
        + \alpha\big(\eps_{\thetav}(\xv_t,t,\mathbf{c})
          - \eps_{\thetav}(\xv_t,t,\mathbf{c}_{\text{neg}})\big)
    \]
    \vskip 4pt
    Now you are steering \emph{away} from a named region as well as toward one.
    \column{0.5\textwidth}
    \begin{center}
      \includegraphics[width=0.94\linewidth]{wl_negative_prompt.jpg}\\[2pt]
      {\scriptsize\color{gray} Welch Labs / 3Blue1Brown (2025)}
    \end{center}
    \vskip -2pt
    \footnotesize
    Wan 2.1's standard negative prompt lists things like extra fingers, deformed limbs, and
    people walking backwards - and is written in Chinese.
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The bill for all this}
  \small
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \column{0.5\textwidth}
    \fcolorbox{armred}{armred!8}{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\small
      \textbf{Guidance spends diversity to buy obedience.}
      \vskip 4pt
      High $\alpha$ concentrates samples in the region the prompt describes most typically.
      Ask for "a cat" at $\alpha=15$ and you get the same few cats.}}
    \column{0.5\textwidth}
    \fcolorbox{armorange}{armorange!12}{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\small
      \textbf{And it doubles the cost.} Each step needs \emph{two} forward passes -
      conditional and unconditional - so a guided 25-step sample is 50 network calls.}}
    \vskip 5pt
    \footnotesize
    Typical values sit around 5-8. Beyond that images get oversaturated and start to look
    the same.
  \end{columns}
  \vskip 6pt
  \footnotesize
  \textcolor{gray}{The spiral showed the far end of this: past a certain $\alpha$ the
  samples stopped resembling the data at all. On real models the same overshoot shows up
  as blown-out colour and hard edges rather than as points leaving a curve.}
\end{frame}

% ================================================================
\begin{frame}{Recap}
  \small
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \column{0.54\textwidth}
    \begin{enumerate}\setlength\itemsep{2pt}
      \item CLIP trains a text and an image encoder into one space, contrastively - pull
        true pairs together, push the rest apart.
      \item The resulting geometry supports arithmetic on concepts, and zero-shot
        classification. But it only maps \emph{into} the space.
      \item Conditioning feeds the prompt to the denoiser - as a \textbf{sequence} of token
        vectors, read by \textbf{cross-attention}.
      \item Conditioning alone is too weak: correct scene, missing subject.
      \item Drop the label sometimes in training, and one model gives both a conditional
        and an unconditional prediction.
      \item Amplify the difference: \textbf{classifier-free guidance}. This is why prompts
        work.
    \end{enumerate}
    \column{0.46\textwidth}
    \vskip 10pt
    \fcolorbox{paramgreen}{paramgreen!8}{\parbox{0.95\linewidth}{\small
      \textbf{Next:} we can now generate what we ask for.
      \vskip 4pt
      But all of this runs in full pixel space, with two network passes per step. At
      $512\times512$ that is far beyond a laptop - and yet Stable Diffusion runs on one.
      \vskip 4pt
      L31: how, plus video, and what all this actually costs.}}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}
