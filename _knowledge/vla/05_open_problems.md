# 05 - What is actually unsolved

Ordered roughly by how much they block progress. Each entry says what is claimed, what is
established, and what would count as evidence that it has been solved.

## 1. Data. Everything else is downstream of this.

**The problem.** Three orders of magnitude between the world's robot data (~300k hours, estimate)
and text (~300T tokens). Robot data is generated by a human moving a robot, roughly one human-hour
per robot-hour, so it does not have an exponential in it.

**What is being tried.** Pooling (Open X-Embodiment), capital (AgiBot's 4,000 square metre
facility, 100 robots, 1M trajectories), simulation and data engines (MimicGen turning 200 human
demos into 50,000), human egocentric video (EgoScale's 20,854 hours), and community collection
(SmolVLA's 487 LeRobot datasets).

**What would count as solved.** A demonstrated scaling law where real-robot performance improves
predictably with a data source that scales without human robot-time.
[EgoScale](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16710) (Feb 2026) is the first serious claim: a log-linear
relationship between human-action prediction loss and human video scale, with that loss predicting
downstream real-robot success. One paper, one lab, one hand morphology. If it replicates, the
constraint changes character; if it does not, human video joins simulation as "helps at the margin".

**The unglamorous version of the same problem.** Nobody knows what makes a demonstration *good*.
The [ICLR 2026 review](https://mbreuss.github.io/blog_post_iclr_26_vla.html) names data quality
and curation as one of the two most under-researched topics in the field, out of 164 submissions.
There is no robotics equivalent of the deduplication-and-filtering literature that drove LLM
quality, and there ought to be.

## 2. Language is barely connected to action

**The problem.** The evidence in `04` is consistent and comes from at least five independent
groups: instructions replaced with `xxx` produce the same trajectory
([LIBERO-PRO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.03827)); models "ignore language instructions completely"
([LIBERO-Plus](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13626)); paraphrases cost 22.8-51.9 points
([LIBERO-Para](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28301)); searched rephrasings cost 93.33% to 5.85%
([DAERT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05595)); non-English costs 30-50%
([multilingual](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11906)); vision overrides language when they conflict
([Fang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17659)).

**Why this is not a minor bug.** The entire justification for putting a *language* model in the
loop is compositional instruction following. If the language channel functions as a task-ID
lookup, then a VLA is an expensive multi-task visuomotor policy, and the pretraining is buying
visual features rather than semantics. [VLM4VLA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03309) points the same
way from the architecture side: the visual module is the bottleneck, not the language one.

**Where the disagreement is.** pi-0.5's ablation reports that **web data mattered most** for
generalizing to novel objects ([Physical Intelligence](https://www.pi.website/blog/pi05)), which
is direct evidence that internet pretraining buys real semantic generalization. Both things can be
true - pretraining supplies open-vocabulary *visual* grounding, while the instruction channel
stays weak - but the field has not cleanly separated them, and the honest position is that we do
not yet know how much of a VLA's generalization is linguistic.

**What would count as solved.** A model whose success rate is flat under DAERT-style adversarial
paraphrase search *and* whose success collapses appropriately when the instruction is replaced
with nonsense. Both directions, together. Nobody has shown this.

## 3. Evaluation is not reproducible, and the benchmarks are saturated

**The problem.** LIBERO is at 95-98% and carries no signal. SIMPLER results on Bridge span 40-99%
across papers, so cross-paper comparison fails. Real-robot evaluation runs at n=10-20 per
condition with **no confidence intervals in any of 13 surveyed standard-practice papers**
([PhAIL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29710)). At those n, differences of 10 points are inside the
noise.

**Why it is uniquely hard here.** The test set is a physical room that no other lab has. Nothing
in the rest of ML has this property.

**What is being tried.** [SureSim](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04354) (large imperfect simulation
plus small real testing, with non-asymptotic interval estimation), real-to-sim reconstruction with
Gaussian splatting so evaluations become shareable artifacts, and distributional reporting
([PhAIL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29710)).

**What would count as solved.** A shared real-robot evaluation that a second lab can reproduce
within stated intervals. Note that this is partly a *sociological* problem: it requires the field
to agree to spend the trials. The datasets survey's conclusion is that progress now depends on
"the co-design of high-fidelity data engines and structured evaluation protocols" more than on
architecture ([survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23001)).

## 4. Long horizons, memory, and recovery

**The problem.** CALVIN five-instruction chains sit at **0.08%** success. Reported experience is
that most VLAs degrade past roughly five minutes of task length. Memory in current robot
foundation models is "a window of recent context, a replay buffer, or retrieval"
([CoRL 2026 memory workshop](https://corl2026-memory.github.io/)) - one documented consequence
being robots that repeat a policy that already failed, because the failure fell out of context.

**Recovery is worse, and it is a data problem, not an architecture problem.** Policies are trained
on successful demonstrations only, so the corrective action was never in the dataset. When the
robot reaches a state the expert never visited, it has nothing to imitate. This is the
compounding-error argument from `01` in its terminal form.

**What is being tried.** Hierarchy (pi-0.5's language subtasks, Gemini Robotics-ER as a separate
planner), world models, and explicit correction data: [pi\*0.6 /
RECAP](https://www.pi.website/blog/pistar06) adds real-time expert corrections plus
advantage-conditioned RL on the robot's own failures. 2026 work like RePO-VLA and Failing Forward
targets the same gap.

**Honest status.** pi\*0.6's espresso robot ran 5:30am to 11:30pm. That is company-reported and it
is a demo, but it is the strongest existing evidence that the recovery problem is *tractable*, and
the mechanism (train on your own mistakes) is the obvious one.

## 5. Latency versus dexterity

**The problem.** A 3-7B forward pass is 50-200 ms. Contact-rich manipulation wants 50-200 Hz.
Chunking buys a factor of H at the cost of reactivity and a discontinuity at every chunk boundary.

**What is being tried, and it is going well.** Dual-system architectures (Helix: 7B at 7-9 Hz
driving 80M at 200 Hz; GR00T N1 the same shape), [real-time
chunking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07339) (compute the next chunk while executing the current,
freeze what has committed, inpaint the rest - training-free, works on any flow/diffusion VLA),
parallel decoding ([OpenVLA-OFT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.19645), 26x), diffusion-LLM backbones
that need one denoising step ([Dream-VLA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.22615), 27x), and
quantization (AUTOQVLA at ICLR 2026: 30% of the original VRAM).

This is the one item on the list where the trajectory is clearly good. It is engineering, and
engineering gets done.

## 6. Cross-embodiment transfer is conditional, not general

**The problem.** Transfer is positive for embodiments with close relatives in the training pool
(reported 80-100% zero-shot between similar arms with similar grippers) and can be *negative* for
those without - the documented cases are Unitree H1 and G1 bipeds, which degraded relative to
isolated training. The reported precondition for negative transfer is large amounts of suboptimal
data combined with high embodiment diversity.

**Why it matters.** "One brain, any robot" is the industry pitch. The measured version is "one
brain, any robot sufficiently similar to something in the training set", which is a much weaker
claim and undercuts the economics.

**What is being tried.** Motion Transfer (Gemini Robotics 1.5), per-embodiment soft prompt tokens
(X-VLA), unified vision-motion codes (XR-1), MoE embodiment adapters, and canonical whole-body
action spaces (LingBot-VLA 2.0's 55 dimensions).

## 7. Safety, and there is no floor under it

This deserves more space than the others because it is the one where the failure mode is not a
bad number.

**The threat model is genuinely different.** [RedVLA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22591) puts it
precisely: risk "has fundamentally shifted from the intent space to the physical space". Nobody has
to jailbreak anything. The instruction stays benign, the scene stays benign-looking, and the
attacker moves a knife. And as [SafeVLA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03480) notes, safety training
in token space does not transfer: a model that refuses to describe knocking over a glass will still
emit the trajectory that knocks it over.

**The measured picture is bad.** 92.7% average attack success rate across five models, 95.5% peak
on pi-0.5, within 10 optimization iterations, gradient-free
([RedVLA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22591)). And [Eva-VLA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.18953)
found **random, non-adversarial** perturbations already cause 33-56% failure. The adversarial case
is worse than the accidental case, but the accidental case is already unacceptable for anything
near a person.

**Three findings that should change how you think about this:**

1. **Capability and vulnerability rise together.** OpenVLA-OFT gained 20.6 points of benign
   success over OpenVLA and 25.6 points of attack success rate. The better policy reaches the
   object more reliably, including when reaching it is the dangerous thing.
2. **The dangerous rollouts are the successful ones.** RedVLA's unsafe rollouts were predominantly
   "Success + Unsafe": the robot completed the task and caused harm on the way. Every metric in
   common use scores these as wins. **Task success rate is not a safety metric and can be
   anti-correlated with one.**
3. **Defenses cost capability.** SimpleVLA-Guard cuts attack success 59.5% and costs 4-10 points
   of benign performance. SafeVLA claims 83.58% cost reduction with **+3.85%** task success, which
   is the more encouraging result, but on its own procedurally generated benchmark.

**What does not exist.** Formal verification of a neural policy. Certification. Any accepted
standard for what a VLA must be tested against before it operates near a person. The practical
consensus in the 2026 literature is layered mitigation - external safety controllers, runtime
monitors, red-team suites - which is an admission that the policy itself cannot be trusted.

**The deployment gap is the thing to say out loud in a lecture.** As of mid-2026 humanoids are in
early commercial deployment (Figure completed an 11-month pilot on BMW production lines; 1X is
positioning NEO for homes) while the research literature reports 92.7% attack success rates on the
same class of model. Those two facts belong on the same slide. Industrial cells with cages and
interlocks are a different risk category from a robot in a kitchen, and the field is moving toward
the kitchen.

## 8. The open-versus-closed capability gap

Open models (OpenVLA, GR00T N1, pi-0.5, Dream-VLA, LingBot-VLA 2.0) match or beat frontier models
on simulation benchmarks. They do not come close on **zero-shot open-world behaviour after
pretraining** - the ICLR 2026 review says academic models "dramatically lag" here, and attributes
it to data quality, poor understanding of demonstration quality, narrow evaluation that rewards
local fine-tuning, and inability to run large real-world trials
([review](https://mbreuss.github.io/blog_post_iclr_26_vla.html)).

The uncomfortable implication for a university course: the benchmarks a student can run do not
measure the capability the field is actually chasing. Say so directly rather than pretending
LIBERO is the frontier.

## 9. Two things the field disagrees about

Report these as open, not settled.

**Do you need a generative action head?** The multimodality argument for diffusion and flow
matching is principled and the reader already has it from ch10. But
[OpenVLA-OFT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.19645) gets 97.1% on LIBERO with **plain L1 regression**
plus chunking and parallel decoding, 26x faster. With action chunking, much of the multimodality
may already be resolved by committing to a chunk. Unresolved.

**Does the "VL" in VLA earn its keep?** For: pi-0.5's ablation says web data mattered most for
novel-object generalization; Octo, trained from scratch on robot data, has weaker open-world
behaviour. Against: [VLM4VLA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03309) finds general VLM capability
poorly predicts control performance and that the *vision* module is the bottleneck; the
linguistic-fragility literature suggests the language pathway is largely unused. The synthesis
that fits all the evidence is that VLM pretraining supplies open-vocabulary visual grounding and
very little compositional language understanding - but that synthesis is mine, not a published
consensus, and should be presented as a hypothesis.

## Honest limits to state in the lecture

- Nothing here is a solved technology. The most capable systems are closed, the most measurable
  systems are open and weaker, and the benchmarks that both report are saturated.
- The most impressive public artifacts are demos. A robot folding laundry for hours is real and
  is not a success rate.
- The measured numbers that do exist are frequently damning: 0.0% on LIBERO-PRO's generalized
  setting, 0.08% on five-step CALVIN, 92.7% attack success under RedVLA, 42.8% real-world success
  after the best sim-to-real recipe in a 10,000-trial study.
- And yet the trajectory is steep. RT-1 was three years and eight months ago and ran at 3 Hz on
  one robot in one kitchen. A list of "current models" written in early 2026 is already partly
  wrong. Any lecture built from this should carry its own date on the first slide.
